# Technical report

What runs, how we measured it, and where it fails. Measured numbers come from `docs/BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`, generated by `python -m eval.run --rerun` (commit `1bca103` at the time of writing), and from `fixloop/`. Requirement IDs (R1–R41) refer to `docs/COMPLIANCE_MATRIX.md`.

## 1. Architecture

```
capture folder ─► tier detection ─► front-end ─► CaptureFrames ─► room geometry ─► stitching ─► damage ─► rules ─► scope ─► plan.json + floorplan.png
                  io/tier_detect    frontends/   schema/          geometry/        stitch/      damage/   configs/*.json      export/

eval/ ◄── tape ground truth (data/ground_truth/), app values (data/app_exports/). The pipeline never reads these.
```

- **One command per capture.** `housefloor run --capture <dir> --tier auto` detects the tier from the folder contents: a Stray Scanner export, `rooms/<room>/photos/`, or a folder holding a video.
- **One contract for every tier.** Each front-end only produces `CaptureFrames`: images, intrinsics, camera poses in metres, optional depth and confidence, and warnings. Everything downstream is shared, so all tiers emit the same JSON, validated against `schemas/floorplan_output.schema.json` on every write.
- **Intervals.** Every measurement is `{value, lo, hi}`, a central 90 % interval (section 4).
- **Evaluation is a separate package.** `eval/` scores a plan against tape ground truth and writes the benchmark report, the README table and the device-matrix summary. The fix loop re-runs the same scoring at two git tags (section 6).

## 2. Tier design and device matrix

**LiDAR (Stray Scanner, Pro iPhone).**
- *Input:* `rgb.mp4`, 256×192 depth maps in millimetres, per-pixel confidence, ARKit poses with per-frame intrinsics (`odometry.csv`) and `camera_matrix.csv`.
- *Fusion:* 60 frames spread evenly over the recording are back-projected using their own intrinsics, rescaled to the depth resolution. Depth is kept between 0.15 and 8 m and filtered by confidence. The cloud is voxelised at 1.8 cm. A hard-surface filter (`pipeline/geometry/hard_surfaces.py`) removes far outliers, which are likely mirror reflections behind walls, and warns and widens intervals when depth is sparse or low-confidence (glass, wet floors, low light).
- *Room model* (`pipeline/geometry/stray_room.py`, after the fix loop):
  - The floor is the 4th percentile of point heights and the cloud top the 96th.
  - The outline is the visible-floor rectangle if it contains at least 90 % of the camera path. Otherwise it is the box around the wall points between floor + 0.35 m and top − 0.25 m, using only points within 1.5 m of the path.
  - The ceiling is the top minus the floor, unless the scan never rose 0.6 m above the camera. In that case it reports the 2.4–3.2 m prior with a wide interval and a warning.
  - Openings are gaps in wall-point coverage along each wall.

**Video.**
- Keyframes are taken at 2 fps behind a Laplacian blur gate, and poses come from optical flow.
- When the video sits next to Stray Scanner odometry (Stray's own `rgb.mp4`), the tier takes metric scale and depth from it. Those results are therefore not video-only.
- A plain iPhone video uses default intrinsics and a fixed flow-to-metres constant, so it has **no metric scale yet**. This is the main gap of the tier.

**Photos.**
- Per room: EXIF intrinsics feed sparse OpenCV multi-view cues (`photo_mvs.py`). Scale comes from how well those cues agree with priors: a door height of 2.0–2.1 m, a camera height of 1.3–1.6 m and a ceiling of 2.4–3.2 m. Each room becomes a rectangle with wide intervals.
- Rooms are placed by a layout solver (`stitch/photo_layout.py`). It pairs doors whose widths agree within 0.25 m and heights within 0.35 m, allows 90° rotations, assumes 0.15 m walls, penalises overlaps, and checks pairings with ORB features on the doorway photos. Folder names are labels only, never adjacency.
- Gap: EXIF in iPhone HEIC files is not read, so with HEIC photos the tier falls back to a prior room. The capture protocol therefore sets the camera to "Most Compatible" (JPEG), which is untested.

**Device matrix** (`docs/DEVICE_MATRIX.md`). Every capture came from one iPhone 13 Pro running iOS 18.7.8. That is in spec for LiDAR and a disclosed deviation for photo and video, where the brief asks for an iPhone 15 or newer. Only the LiDAR tier is measured. The pipeline reads intrinsics from the capture files and never branches on the device model.

## 3. Drift handling

- **One frame.** LiDAR and video captures are a single continuous recording in one world frame. The protocol ends the walk at the start mark.
- **Correction** (`--drift on`, the default; `pipeline/geometry/stray_poses.py`). The start-to-end position gap is spread linearly along the trajectory, scaled by min(0.55, gap ÷ path span), so later poses move most. On B1 the gap was 6.5 cm. The correction is translation-only, with no ICP or pose graph, and rotational drift is not corrected.
- **Multi-room.** Stray captures are split into rooms by time segments declared in a manifest (`tiers/stray_segment.py`), placed in the shared frame, and stitched with overlap checks.
- **Ablation.** `housefloor ablate-drift` and `ablate-video-drift` compute the stitched footprint with correction on and off. We have no multi-room capture with tape truth, so the ablation is not yet reported against ground truth (R22, partial).

## 4. Error budget

Each measurement's σ starts from a per-tier constant (`pipeline/config.py`):

| Tier | Length | Opening | Height | Footprint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LiDAR | 0.8 cm | 1.5 cm | 1.2 cm | 1 % |
| Video | 2.5 cm | 3 cm | 2.5 cm | 3 % |
| Photos | 6 cm | 8 cm | 5 cm | 8 % |

The geometry code raises these where the capture is weaker. LiDAR walls use max(0.8 cm, 2 cm + 0.5 × the capture's relative scale σ). Areas use the larger of the tier percentage and twice the relative scale σ. Thin input (too few wall points, an unseen ceiling) widens σ further. The interval half-width is 1.645 × k × σ, where k is a calibration multiplier.

These constants are engineering estimates covering sensor noise, plane-fit residual and scale. They are not an error budget propagated per measurement from separate sources, which the brief's design called for (§5.9), and we do not claim one.

## 5. Calibration analysis

- **Fit.** The multipliers k in `configs/calibration.json` were fitted on a synthetic two-room fixture (`eval/calibrate.py`), not on measured rooms; the photo and video values are hand-set.
- **Coverage on B1** (tape):
  - All six room measurements lie inside their 90 % intervals: four walls, ceiling and floor area.
  - Both matched door widths lie outside theirs, because those detections are phantoms or mismatches.
  - That gives 6 of 8 overall.
- **What it tells us.** With one measured room we cannot fit k per tier and measurement type; the brief's leave-one-room-out needs several rooms. Geometry coverage of 6 of 6 suggests the LiDAR geometry intervals are not too narrow on B1, but one room proves little. Opening intervals are clearly too narrow for the detection errors we see.
- **Thin input.** An unseen ceiling now reports the 2.4–3.2 m prior with a wide interval. Before the fix loop it was a tight interval around a clamped 2.2 m, which is exactly the confident-garbage case the brief warns about.

## 6. Results on B1 and the fix loop

**B1** is a furnished bedroom, tape-measured, captured with Stray Scanner in 211 s (3,165 frames) and processed in 13 s on a Windows laptop CPU.
- **Room geometry:** walls are −1.4 cm (short) and −3.9 cm (long), the ceiling +1.1 cm and the floor area −1.0 %. Four of six strict gates pass; the long walls miss ±2 cm by 1.9 cm.
- **Openings:** 0 of 4 taped openings within 2 cm, with 6 phantom doors, and both windows missed.
- **Head-to-head with magicplan 2026.38.0:** magicplan's values were read off the app, because its export needs a paid plan. We beat it on both long walls and the ceiling. It is closer on the short walls and the door and window widths. That is 3 of 7, below the 70 % gate. Floor area: ours −1.0 %, magicplan −1.7 %.

**Fix loop** (`fixloop/`, regenerable with `bash fixloop/run.sh`).
1. **The regression.** A rewrite of the LiDAR geometry, tuned on the company samples, collapsed B1 to a 0.67 × 2.77 m room with a 2.20 m ceiling. That is a 92.4 % area error.
2. **The declaration.** We tagged the benchmark `fixloop-before` and committed the declaration before any fix. It named the gate and the failing numbers and gave a root-cause hypothesis: an outline built from the visible floor collapses when furniture hides it, and the ceiling percentile hides behind a 2.2 m clamp. Bisecting pinned the change to one commit, and the declaration predicted per-measurement numbers.
3. **The fix** (tag `fixloop-after`). Keep the floor outline only if it contains the camera path, otherwise fall back to the wall box with a reach limit for points seen through openings, and remove the clamp.
4. **Results.** Floor area, short walls and ceiling now pass, and the long walls miss by 1.9 cm. Three of four predictions landed in range; the ceiling came in 0.6 cm above it.
5. **What we missed.** `fit_floor_plane` accepts planes of any orientation and had returned a wall as the floor. We found this during the fix and wrote it up in the post-mortem; the fix no longer depends on that fit.

## 7. Known failure modes

- **Rotated rooms.** The single-room box is aligned to the capture's world axes, so a room rotated relative to the start heading is overestimated (seen on the company `with_ceiling` sample). Two estimators for room axes failed on B1's clutter. The protocol mitigates it by starting square to a wall.
- **Openings.** Gaps where furniture hides a wall become phantom doors, and windows are not detected. Openings are the weakest gate and the next fix-loop candidate.
- **Floor-plane fit.** It accepts walls (see section 6). This is contained, not fixed.
- **Photo tier with HEIC** falls back to a prior. **Video without odometry** has no metric scale.
- **Captures spanning several rooms without a manifest** are treated as one room, giving very large outlines (the company `floor_only` and `with_ceiling` samples).
- **Mirrors, glass, wet-look floors, low light.**
  - Far outliers (likely mirror reflections) are removed.
  - Sparse or low-confidence depth (glass, wet floors, low light) triggers a warning and wider intervals.
  - Video drops blurry frames.
  - None of this has been tested on purpose-made captures.
- **Damage.**
  - Detection is heuristic, with MobileSAM refinement only when PyTorch is installed (not in the default install), and projection onto walls through the camera.
  - It has not been validated, because no room with staged damage was captured.
  - Concealed-damage flags and scope follow fixed rules (`configs/concealed_rules.json`, `configs/scope_rules.json`).
- **Company samples.** They came without ground truth, so their outputs (`outputs/company_samples/`) show the pipeline runs end to end, not how accurate it is.

## 8. Constraints

- **Capture:** handheld consumer capture only, with a stock app (Stray Scanner) and the iPhone Camera app (Route 2, `docs/CAPTURE_PROTOCOL.md`).
- **Offline:** every reported result was produced offline. The optional Azure OpenAI hooks would call our own Azure deployment, so they stay off.
- **Weights and data:** large files are not in git. `scripts/fetch_weights.py` downloads optional weights, and `scripts/fetch_data.py` downloads captures listed with sha256 in `data/manifest.csv`.
- **Disclosure** of models, apps and APIs is in the README.
